# Trading In a Car

The trade is a separate negotiation from the price of the new car, and dealers
love to blur the two so they can give with one hand and take with the other.
Keep them apart.

## Know your real trade value — with cash offers, not KBB

**Throw out Kelley Blue Book** (and JD Power / NADA guides) for valuing your
trade. Since ~2020, prices move too fast for those guides to keep up — their data
can be weeks stale, so they're often off by thousands in either direction.

Get **actual cash offers** instead: **Carvana, CarMax, and Vroom** (whichever
operate in the user's area). These are real, cash-in-hand offers, usually at or
above market, and they're your negotiating floor. When you sit down with the
dealer, that number is the *minimum* you'll accept for the trade — if the dealer
can't match or beat it, you sell to Carvana/CarMax instead.

**Account for the tax credit.** In most states, trading in reduces the sales tax
you pay on the new car (you're taxed only on the difference). So a dealer's trade
offer plus the tax savings can beat a straight Carvana sale even at a slightly
lower headline number. Do that math before deciding where the trade goes.

Also worth telling the user honestly: **selling it yourself (private party) almost
always nets the most** — especially on older cars a dealer will barely value.
Trade-in wins on convenience, safety, and the tax credit; private sale wins on
dollars. Their call.

## Maximize the offer

- **When filling out the Carvana/CarMax questionnaire, describe the car as in
  great condition.** These services only ever *lower* an offer after inspection,
  never raise it. Start at the top number; let them find issues and adjust down.
  They will not look at a car and volunteer more than you claimed.
- **Don't volunteer your car's flaws to the dealer.** It's their job to appraise
  it — the silent walk-around, the plug-in diagnostic. You don't need to announce
  that the check-engine light was on last week (if it's off now, that's their
  problem to find). *Do* disclose things they'll find anyway and that are material
  and legal to disclose — a salvage/branded title, for instance. But don't do the
  dealer's devaluation work for them.

## Negotiate the trade last, and watch "over-allowing"

- **Settle the price of the new car first**, as if there's no trade at all. Only
  once that number is locked do you bring in the trade. This stops the dealer from
  shuffling money between the two to make a bad deal look good.
- **Watch over-allowing.** Sometimes a dealer will give you *more* than your car
  is worth on the trade to distract from giving you no discount on the new car
  ("we can't move on price, but look how much we're giving you for your trade!").
  Because you already have accurate Carvana/CarMax numbers, you'll spot it. If
  their trade number is inflated to hide a weak car price, consider pulling the
  trade out of the deal entirely and selling it separately.

## The one-line summary

Get cash offers (Carvana/CarMax/Vroom), treat that as your floor, negotiate the
car's price with the trade set aside, then bring the trade in at the end and make
sure the dealer at least matches your cash offer (accounting for tax credit).
