# Getting Quotes & Running the Competition

This is the engine of the whole method. Everything else supports it. The goal is
to make dealerships bid against each other for the user's business, entirely over
text/email/phone, until nobody will go lower.

## The loop

1. **Cast a wide net.** Contact many dealerships that have the car — the whole
   metro, a 2-hour radius, and wider (even nationwide) if the car is rare or the
   local market won't budge. Roughly 20% of dealers simply won't negotiate well;
   the only way to route around them is volume. If there are 40 dealers with the
   car, plenty of them will play ball.

2. **Ask every one for the best out-the-door (OTD) price with a full breakdown.**
   Out-the-door means the final number including every fee. The breakdown matters
   because that's where add-ons hide (see `dealer-tactics.md`).

3. **Take the best quote to everyone else and ask them to beat it.** Send it to
   the dealers who quoted higher *and* the ones who never quoted. "I've got X
   out-the-door on this exact car — can you beat it?" Keep cycling the best number
   back through the field.

4. **Repeat until nobody will beat it by a meaningful amount.** That floor is the
   deal. If a dealer beats it by $5, that's not worth switching over — reward the
   dealer who's been easy to work with.

## Why online/phone/text, always

- **The showroom is the dealer's home turf.** In person they can read your
  emotions, apply pressure, run the clock, and separate you from your resolve.
  Over text you can pause, think, get advice, and keep your cards hidden.
- **Text creates useful silence.** A gap in a text thread reads to the dealer as
  "they might be buying elsewhere right now," which often pulls out a better offer
  on its own. Use the pause.
- **A dealer who won't give numbers over the phone won't give a good deal in
  person.** Treat that refusal as information: it's a filter that saves you a
  wasted drive. There are always other dealers who will.

## Creating urgency (honestly)

The single most powerful thing a buyer can convey: **"I'm buying a car today or
tomorrow. I've already decided on the car — I'm just deciding *where* to buy it."**

A serious, near-term buyer is the hottest lead a salesperson has, and it moves
you to the front of the line for the best numbers. You don't actually have to
buy that instant — once you have the numbers you can say you'll come by later or
tomorrow. But the urgency is what earns the competitive quote in the first place.

Pair it with competition every time: "I'm getting numbers from several dealers
and going with the best out-the-door price."

## Getting the salesperson on your side

Be genuinely nice. Learn their name, make small talk, keep it light. A
salesperson who likes you becomes your advocate — this is called "working your
manager": they go argue with the desk for a better price *on your behalf*. Being
a Karen gets you held at MSRP; sometimes dealers deliberately won't discount for
a rude customer (partly because of CSI — customer-satisfaction survey scores that
affect the dealership). Warm and firm beats aggressive every time.

## Locking the deal before you drive in

A verbal quote or a screenshot is not a binding number. Numbers "change" between
the phone call and the desk all the time. Before committing to a drive:

- **Get a final OTD breakdown in writing**, line by line, with no surprise
  add-ons.
- **Ideally get a manager-signed worksheet (a "T-sheet")** — the most official
  numbers you can get short of the contract.
- **Watch the bait-and-switch.** A common move: the dealer "removes" the add-on
  fees you objected to, then quietly raises the price of the car to make it back.
  Always confirm the *final* out-the-door number and that the car price itself
  didn't move. Get it in writing that the discount stands and no fees crept back.

## Handling the "just come in" stalls

Dealers will try to pull you into the store instead of quoting. The counters live
in `dealer-tactics.md` and the ready-to-send versions in `message-templates.md`,
but the through-line is always the same: *"I already know the car I want, I'm
buying in the next day or two, I just need your best out-the-door price — other
dealers are giving me numbers, and I'll reward the one who does."*
