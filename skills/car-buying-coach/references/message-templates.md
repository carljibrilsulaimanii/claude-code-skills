# Message Templates

Adaptable openers, follow-ups, and counters for the user to send dealerships.
**Never send these raw** — always fill in the real car, trim, stock/VIN, the
user's city, and their actual numbers, and trim the tone to sound like the user.
The voice is warm, low-pressure, and quietly immovable (see the house-style note
in SKILL.md).

When the user pastes in what a salesperson said, match it to a counter here (or
in `dealer-tactics.md`) and hand them a message they can send as-is.

## First contact (get a quote)

> Hi [name], I'm interested in the [year make model trim] you have — stock #
> [####] / VIN ending [####]. I've already driven one and know it's the car I
> want, so I don't need to come in to look. I'm buying in the next day or two and
> just comparing out-the-door prices from a few dealers. What's your best
> out-the-door number, with a full breakdown of fees? Whoever's most competitive
> gets my business today or tomorrow.

Why it works: names the exact car (serious buyer), states urgency, signals
competition, and asks for OTD with a breakdown so add-ons can't hide.

## They say "come in" / "presence is power"

> I hear you, and I appreciate it — but I'm not comfortable coming in until I've
> got all the numbers in front of me. A few other dealers are giving me
> out-the-door prices over text, and honestly I'd rather reward the dealership
> that's upfront with numbers. If you can send your best out-the-door price, I'm
> ready to move today or tomorrow. If not, I completely understand.

Then go quiet. The silence often pulls out a better number on its own.

## They say "we don't give numbers over the phone"

> No worries at all — I get that it's not how every store works. I work about
> [14]-hour days and don't have time to sit at a dealership for hours, so I'm
> getting numbers from several dealers and buying from whoever's most competitive.
> If that's not something you can do, I totally understand and I'll work with the
> others. Thanks either way!

If they still won't, move on — it's a filter, not a wall.

## They say "this is my best price"

> Appreciate it! I've actually got a lower out-the-door quote in hand right now,
> so I'll probably go that direction — but if you can beat it, I'd love to give
> you the deal. Either way, thanks for your time.

Two outcomes: they ask to see the quote (they'll go lower), or they let you walk
(it was their floor — and they often call back the next day).

## Taking the best quote back to the field

> Quick update — I've got [$XX,XXX] out-the-door on this exact [model], no add-on
> fees. I'd genuinely rather buy from you. Can you beat it? If you can, I'll lock
> it in today.

Send to the dealers who quoted higher *and* the ones who never sent a number.

## Getting add-ons removed

> Thanks for the breakdown. I see [tint / nitrogen / paint protection /
> "protection package"] on there. I'm happy to keep the [tint, since I want it],
> but I can't pay for the rest — I've got quotes from other dealers with none of
> these add-ons. I'm fine with the discount you're offering if we take the add-ons
> off. Can you do that?

Then confirm the final number so they don't quietly raise the car price to make
it back:

> Perfect — just to confirm, the out-the-door is now [$XX,XXX], the discount
> stands, and there are no add-on fees, correct? Happy to come in once I've got
> that in writing.

## Interest rate — asking them to beat your pre-approval

> On financing — I'm pre-approved at [X.XX]% through my credit union, which I'm
> happy with. If you can beat it by at least a half a percent, I'll finance
> through you. If not, I'll just use my pre-approval. Either way works for me.

## Pinning down GAP or a warranty price

> On the GAP — I know what a fair number looks like. I'll do [$450]. If that
> works, great; if not, I'll pick it up elsewhere. Can you make it happen?

(Same shape for an extended warranty, e.g., "I'll do $1,500," and stay firm.)

## Locking numbers before the drive

> Before I make the drive, can you send me the final out-the-door breakdown signed
> by a manager? I just want the numbers to match when I get there — I've had
> quotes change at the desk before, so a signed worksheet gives me confidence to
> come in and sign.

## Creating urgency when a deal is stalling

> Just so you know where I'm at — I'm ready to buy today or tomorrow, I've decided
> on the car, and I'm talking to a few dealers. I'd love it to be you, but if we
> can't get to a number I'll go with whoever can. No pressure either way.

## Walking away (leaving the door open)

> Totally understand — sounds like we're not going to line up on price this time.
> I appreciate your time and the straight answers. If anything changes on your end
> in the next day or two, shoot me a number and I'll take a look.

Dealers fear a lost sale; a friendly walk-away frequently earns a callback.
