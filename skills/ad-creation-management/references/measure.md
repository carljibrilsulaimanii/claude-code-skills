# Measure and learn (Stage 5)

Read results honestly, decide what happens to each ad, and turn what was
learned into the next round of angles and copy. The budget call itself
(how much more or less to spend) belongs to whoever owns the budget
decision; this stage decides what to make next and hands them the evidence.

## How a winner is judged

- **Purchases, not leads.** The goal is revenue in your payment processor.
  Rank ads and angles on first-touch purchases per $1,000 spent and revenue
  per ad dollar, from your CRM's first-visit tracking joined to payments.
  Cost per lead explains why value moved; it is not the scoreboard. The
  cheapest lead source is often the worst buyer source.
- **Live-pitch days are the pitch, not the ad.** If you sell on a live
  webinar or event, event days can sell several times a normal day. A spike
  in platform-attributed purchases on an event day is mostly the pitch; never
  cite it as proof one ad earned more budget. First-touch purchases already
  include the pitch's sales from the people that ad brought in, so do not
  add the pitch effect on top.
- **Only settled data.** Meta days close in the account's time zone and the
  latest couple of hours are still filling. Google conversions can take about
  three days to settle. Today is always partial.
- **Enough evidence.** Set a spend floor in the blueprint (a few times your
  target cost per purchase); below it an ad or angle is untested, win or
  lose. With a small number of purchases, give a range, not a point.
- **Label every source.** Platform-attributed, CRM first-touch and checkout
  figures are different rulers and are never mixed in one comparison, or in
  one reply about several campaigns.

## The three moves

1. **Scale what works.** Hand the case to whoever owns the budget, with the
   evidence and a small step size. This stage only says which angle and
   creative earned the case.
2. **Replace what does not.** First ask why. Low delivery means untested, not
   failed. If the angle had a fair test and lost, retire the angle. If the
   angle is sound but the execution lost, keep the angle and write new hooks
   or make new creative for it. Replace with a new ad, never by editing a
   running top performer just before a big sales day.
3. **Test new angles.** Pull the next candidates from the Creative Mosaic
   backlog, choosing distinct hypotheses, not ten versions of one message.
   Each new test gets a written test question and a stop rule before it
   launches.

## The learning log

Keep it in the blueprint, one row per ad: creative label, Meta ad ID, angle
ID, copy IDs, awareness stage, objection or desire, format, result window,
spend, first-touch purchases, outcome (scale, keep, replace, retire,
untested) and confidence. Small samples never become permanent rules.

Feed it back into the earlier stages:

- **Stage 1**: new objections from comments, SMS replies and inbox
  conversations go into the evidence register and the objection map; angles
  that won or lost update their cards.
- **Stage 2**: hooks and lines that won become voice references; retired copy
  is marked retired in the copy bank.
- **Stage 3**: formats and styles that held up update the Video Style Card
  and the static references.
- Close each cycle with the next three briefs: angle, why it is next, the test
  question, and the proof it needs.

## Reporting rules

- Default window: last 7 complete days against the 7 before, in the account's
  time zone.
- Show scope, currency, time zone, attribution setting, date range and
  retrieval time with every number.
- Recompute rates from summed numerators and denominators; never average
  rates across rows.
- When summing amounts from an API search, check the rows you summed equal
  the reported total. Summing one capped page while quoting the full count
  understates revenue.
- Keep purchases and leads apart; never blend them into one cost per result.
- Revenue always names its source: platform-attributed value, checkout, or
  total business revenue. Never add them together.
- Missing data has an honest label: Data needed, Unavailable, Not applicable,
  Not calculable. Blank is not zero, and zero purchases is not a $0 cost per
  purchase.

## The Performance Ledger (optional dashboard)

When a visual report helps, build it as an HTML page from retrieved data
only:

- **Performance**: cost per 1,000 impressions, link click-through rate,
  landing-page conversion rate, cost per result, return on ad spend, revenue,
  with the supporting totals.
- **Campaigns**: objective, delivery state, budget, spend, results, cost per
  result.
- **Creative**: each ad's label, angle ID, status, spend, results, cost per
  result, first-touch purchases, and its outcome from the learning log.
- Charts from real data only: spend and revenue over time, daily results,
  cost per result against the agreed target. Gaps stay gaps.
- "What this means" at the bottom: one verdict (fix now, wait, keep, test,
  replace, review for scaling), up to three observations kept separate from
  their possible explanations, and up to three next steps.
- Put checkout revenue next to the platform's attributed value, each
  labeled. What-if numbers live in a separate, labeled view.
