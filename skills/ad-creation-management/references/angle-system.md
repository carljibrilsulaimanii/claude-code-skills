# The angle system (Stage 1)

How to turn research into a testable backlog of ad angles.

## Words to define for the reader the first time they appear

- **Angle**: one distinct reason a specific buyer should change a belief,
  want the outcome, trust the approach, or take the next step.
- **Hook**: the opening words or seconds that express an angle. Many hooks
  can share one angle.
- **Format**: image, carousel, talking head, b-roll. Never counts as a new
  angle.
- **Awareness stage**: how much the buyer already knows about the problem
  and the ways to solve it.
- **Mechanism**: the distinctive way the offer gets its result. Name it only
  from what the offer really does.
- **Market sophistication**: the promises this market has heard so often
  they no longer land.

## Evidence register

Every source gets an ID (S01, S02, ...) with: link or file, date observed,
type, the relevant excerpt or paraphrase, fit to the buyer, limits. Keep four
classes apart and never let one stand in for another:

1. Customer evidence (a real buyer's words or behavior).
2. Competitor claim (what a seller says about itself).
3. Practitioner guidance (useful as a checklist, not as proof of what your
   buyers think).
4. Your hypothesis.

The best customer evidence is usually in-house: SMS and email replies, the
support or sales inbox, webinar chat questions, refund reasons, and
sales-call notes. Pull these before reading any competitor.

For competitor ads record advertiser, ad link, date seen, country filter,
hook, core argument, format, proof shown, offer, CTA and landing page. A
long-running ad is a clue, not proof it is profitable. Never invent anyone's
spend, targeting or results.

## Objection families (a checklist, not a finding)

Investigate each and keep only what the evidence supports, written in the
buyer's actual situation:

- cannot pay now vs unsure it is worth it (keep these apart)
- hidden total cost: upsells, tools, ad spend, renewals
- no reason to act now; something else is more urgent
- not ready yet; a prerequisite comes first
- no time; workload unclear
- too technical or too many moving parts
- doubts own follow-through after past unfinished courses
- "my situation is different from the people in the testimonials"
- does not understand why the method works, or thinks it is recycled
- does not know or trust the teacher, or who actually delivers it
- proof looks exceptional, or founder results are passed off as customer
  results
- burned before by a similar purchase
- fears being left alone when stuck
- format and logistics: live vs recorded, time zone, access length
- free content or AI seems good enough
- dislikes the tactics or the persona they would have to adopt
- privacy: does not want to be on camera, in a group, or judged
- a spouse, partner or co-owner has to agree
- fine print: refunds, cancellation, commitment

If the funnel has a free step before the paid one (a webinar, a class, a
trial), separate **resistance to the free step** from **objections to the
purchase**. They are different decisions with different answers. Do not
shame price concerns, manufacture urgency, or assume a buyer's family
situation.

Map positive motivations too: practical results, relief, confidence,
mastery, independence, belonging, providing for family, identity. A mosaic of
50 rebuttals misses half the reasons people buy.

## Buyer identity

For each buyer situation, record only what evidence supports:

- **Current**: how they see themselves now.
- **Aspired**: who they want to become.
- **Rejected**: who they refuse to be or what they will not do.

## The 50 angle cards

Default awareness split (adjust only with a stated reason, total stays 50):

| Starting awareness | Cards |
|---|---|
| Unaware | 8 |
| Problem-aware | 15 |
| Solution-aware | 15 |
| Offer-aware | 7 |
| Most-aware | 5 |

Also tag **brand relationship**: new to the brand, or already exposed. A cold
viewer can be solution-aware already. Do not mark every cold viewer unaware.

The 23 unaware and problem-aware cards do the teaching: they name the
problem, say why the usual approaches fail, and earn attention before any
offer appears. Each must pass the **cold stranger test**: someone who has
never heard of the brand reads the hook and knows exactly what is offered,
to whom, and why.

Portfolio balance to check:

- at least 10 logic-led and 10 emotion-led cards; every card names both its
  emotional driver and its logical proof
- all four value drivers covered: the dream outcome, the believed chance of
  getting it, how long it takes, how much effort or sacrifice it costs
- at least 12 desire-led and 12 objection-answering cards
- 3 to 6 real buyer situations, not invented personas

Useful patterns to draw from: mechanism reveal, myth-busting lesson, "even
if" (it works even under the buyer's specific constraint), cost of waiting,
"this is not for you if", identity ("for the person who..."), demonstration,
comparison with the alternatives, direct FAQ answer, story with proof, risk
reversal only when a real guarantee exists.

Each card carries:

1. ID and angle name, with the argument in one or two sentences
2. buyer situation and what triggers the search
3. starting awareness and brand relationship
4. appeal (logic, emotion, blend) with emotional driver and logical proof
5. value driver
6. objection ID or desire it serves
7. belief shift (and identity shift where relevant): what they think now,
   what the ad must make credible
8. one draft hook in the brand voice, labeled draft
9. proof needed, with status AVAILABLE / NEEDS CONFIRMATION / MISSING
10. execution idea and a CTA that matches the real destination
11. evidence IDs, and whether the card is evidence-informed or a hypothesis

## Audit before delivery

- 50 cards, unique IDs, awareness counts add to 50.
- Duplicates removed: same buyer + same belief shift + same argument is one
  card, whatever the wording or format.
- Every card passes the cold stranger test or is flagged.
- Every factual claim traced to evidence; missing proof listed as a
  production dependency.
- Invented buyer thoughts are never presented as quotes.
- Coverage counts shown by awareness, appeal, buyer situation, value driver,
  motivation and objection; gaps explained.
- Saturated claims without a distinct mechanism flagged.
- No competitor names in public-facing hooks; no "it's not X, it's Y"
  filler; no confidence scores or "viral" predictions.
- Top 10 to produce first, chosen for buyer relevance, evidence, credible
  difference, teaching value for cold traffic, mechanism clarity, available
  proof, ease of production and variety. Each gets one test question. They
  are tests, not predicted winners.

## Deliverables

- **Creative Mosaic**: interactive HTML page. 50 tiles colored by
  awareness, filters for awareness, appeal, buyer situation, objection family
  and value driver that combine, click to open the full card, and a second
  view clustering objections with their angle IDs and proof status. Count
  the tiles before presenting.
- **Positioning map**: HTML page with three toggles (time to first result
  by depth of method; ease for the buyer by size of outcome; total sacrifice
  by perceived value). The offer is highlighted, competitors are fair
  one-line descriptions, a takeaway box names the crowded and open
  quadrants, and a footnote says positions are judgment calls, not measured
  data.
- **One-page snapshot**: offer in one line, who it is for, the mechanism,
  top three angles with hooks.
- **Competitor swipe file**: private. Never shared outside the team as is.
- **Blueprint update** with the approved position and angle IDs.

## Claims and policy (regulated offers)

For offers about money, investing, health, housing, credit or employment:

- No promised returns, income, or "replace your salary" figures. Results
  shown need context and must be real; founder results are labeled as such.
- No "guaranteed", "risk-free", or deadline language unless the offer truly
  has it.
- Real-estate, credit and employment creative can be limited to restricted
  targeting (Meta's Special Ad Categories, Google's personalized-advertising
  policies). Plan for the audience restriction rather than fighting it in
  copy.
- Google disapproves big promises as "Unreliable claims". When a hook makes
  a big promise, pair it with the method, or cut it.
