# Writing the ads (Stage 2)

Turn approved angles into approved words before anyone spends time or credits
making images or video. Copy approved here is reused unchanged in Stage 3 and
Stage 4.

## Inputs (from the blueprint)

- Approved angle IDs with their argument, buyer situation, awareness stage,
  belief shift and proof status.
- The offer, the destination page (full URL) and the action it asks for.
- Voice: one to three writing samples, or a dictated one-minute explanation of
  the offer in the speaker's own words, plus any words or phrases never used.
- Approved claims and proof. Anything marked MISSING stays out of the copy.

## Calibrate the voice first

Write one short passage for one angle, ask what sounds off (too formal, too
pushy, too long, words never used), fix it, and get it approved. Only then
write the rest. Record the approved sample and the banned words in the
blueprint's voice section so later batches start calibrated.

## What to write for each angle

| Piece | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Hooks (3 per angle) | Three genuinely different openings for the same argument: the buyer's situation, the belief being challenged, a specific fact or demonstration. Same argument, different door. |
| Primary text, short | The hook plus one or two lines and the next step. The first line has to work alone, since most people see only that before "more". |
| Primary text, long | Hook, the problem, why the usual approach falls short, the mechanism, proof, the next step. For warmer audiences and for testing against the short version. |
| Headline | A few words that say what happens next ("Free class Thursday, 7 PM ET"), not a second hook. |
| Description | Optional; a short supporting line where the placement shows it. |
| CTA button | Must match the destination, for example `SIGN_UP` for a free registration, `BUY_TICKETS` for a ticket page, `LEARN_MORE` for content. |
| On-image text | One idea, a handful of words, readable on a phone. Never a paragraph. |
| Video script | For each video route: hook in the first three seconds, the problem or belief, the mechanism, one proof point, a specific next step. Write the timing per beat and the on-screen captions. Give a 15-second and a 30 to 60-second cut when the route allows it. |

Label every piece with a copy ID tied to its angle: `A012-H1` to `A012-H3`
for hooks, `A012-PS` and `A012-PL` for short and long primary text, `A012-HL`
for the headline, `A012-V15` for a 15-second script.

## Checks before the copy goes for approval

- **Cold stranger test** on every hook for cold audiences: someone who has
  never heard of the brand knows what is offered, to whom, and why.
- **Claims match proof.** Nothing stated as fact unless its proof is
  AVAILABLE. No income, return or "replace your salary" promises. No
  "guaranteed" or "risk-free" unless the offer truly has it.
- **Do not describe the viewer's personal circumstances.** Meta's ad policy
  forbids asserting or implying someone's personal attributes, which includes
  their financial situation and health. "Struggling with debt?" is out; "A
  plan for paying down debt" is fine.
- **Dates and times match reality.** Event day, date and time come from the
  team's source of truth (the event calendar or tracker) and the live
  registration page, never from memory. Re-check both before approval.
- **Destination fit.** Copy for an ad landing on a ticket or checkout page
  sells that offer directly. Copy for a free registration sells the free
  step, not the paid offer behind it.
- **House style.** Whatever the blueprint records (for example plain
  language, no emojis, no em dashes), plus no competitor names in anything
  public and no "it's not X, it's Y" filler.

## Approval gate and handoff

The user approves the words per angle. Record each piece in the blueprint's
copy bank with its copy ID, angle ID, destination, status (draft, approved,
retired) and approval date. Stage 3 builds creative only from approved copy,
and any later edit to approved wording goes back through this stage.
